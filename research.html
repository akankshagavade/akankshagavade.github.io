Human-Centered Computing: Empirical Studies in HCI

First Author, ACM COMPASS '23 · Presented virtually, South Africa · with Dr. Rama Adithya Varanasi (NYU) and Dr. Nicola Dell, Cornell University

Published

What

Technology is generally built to improve productivity and reduce stress. However, in low-resource, low-income settings, sometimes technology itself becomes source of stress. During the pandemic, the shift to hybrid education caused stress in teachers in low-income schools in India. That said, technology also served as a medium or source of support to relieve said stress. This study presents design recommendations for a tech-based support solution.

Methods

  • Thematic coding of 28 multilingual interviews (Hindi, Marathi, English) with teachers and school management
  • Literature Synthesis to build the theoretical framework and support cross-cultural analysis of technology, social support, organizational structures and well-being.

Identifying the Types of Feedback That Users Provide During the Design of Data-Driven Algorithmic Systems

Under review at IJHCI '26

Under Review

What

Users provide valuable feedback when it comes to designing algorithmic systems. However, it is important to know what users comment on, how they express it and how the format of engagement itself affects what feedback is received. Using the data collected from five years of participatory design, we can now identify and close the gap between what feedback designers expect vs what feedback users actually provide.

Methods

  • Semi-structured Interviews
  • Speculative Workshops
  • Think Aloud Sessions

Entrepreneurial Well-Being Across Cultures

Ruhr Fellowship · Technical University of Dortmund, Germany · Summer 2025

What

Entrepreneurship looks different depending on where you are, and so does the toll it takes on the people doing it. This study compares the well-being needs of entrepreneurs across three different startup ecosystems: Ruhr, Lehigh, and Silicon Valley. How do entrepreneurs and innovators experience and manage well-being in the context of their work, across different cultural, institutional, and economic settings? We aim to understand what’s working, what challenges persist, and what kinds of support systems could truly make a difference.

Methods

  • Literature synthesis of 50+ academic papers to build a theoretical framework and inform a grant submission
  • Participant recruitment via snowball sampling and cold outreach, resulting in 10 interviews
  • Semi-structured Interviews and Analysis, synthesizing findings for academic and institutional stakeholders

Framing, Expectations, and Trust in Algorithmic Advice

Independent study · with Dr. Felipe Augusto de Araujo · January 2026 - May 2026

What

People often stop trusting an AI's advice the moment it makes a mistake, but it's unclear how much that depends on how the AI was described beforehand. This study tests whether calling an AI a "learning" system makes people more forgiving of an early error than calling it fixed, and whether setting high expectations upfront makes a mistake feel like a bigger betrayal.

Methods

  • Literature synthesis across algorithm aversion, prospect theory, and expectation violation theory to build the study's hypotheses
  • 2×2 experimental design manipulating AI labeling (learning vs. static) and expectation framing (high vs. low)
