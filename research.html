Human-Centered Computing: Empirical Studies in HCI

ACM COMPASS '23 · Presented virtually, South Africa · with Dr. Nicola Dell, Cornell University

Published

Abstract

This study explores how low-income school teachers coped with increased isolation and stress during the shift to hybrid education post-COVID. As first author, I analyzed and thematically coded 28 multilingual interviews (Hindi, Marathi, and English) with teachers and management, and co-developed the study's theoretical framework around how teachers built different types of support systems to combat stress.

How Teacher Feedback Helped Reimagine, Redesign, and Recode a Sustainability Education App

Global Social Impact Fellowship 2023 · Supervised by Dr. Khanjan Mehta · Save Tuba project

Best Paper Award · GHTC '23

Abstract

Human-centered design is essential when developing technologies aimed at improving people's lives. This paper details how we used feedback from teacher focus groups to enhance our approach to addressing environmental problems and to build a better educational-gaming application. I led the end-to-end qualitative research: running and synthesizing 6 focus groups with 30+ users across Kazakh- and Russian-medium schools during field deployment in Kazakhstan, then translating those findings into new gamification and inquiry-based learning features for the roadmap.

Semantic Search over 3,800+ Legal Case Files

Data, Algorithms and Society Lab, Lehigh University · with Dr. Eric Baumer · Peer-reviewed at CSCW '26

Peer-Reviewed

Abstract

Working as a research assistant, I synthesized qualitative findings into product recommendations that informed UX specifications for search, filtering, navigation, and information architecture across a repository of 3,800+ legal case files. I designed interview protocols and thematically coded 40+ semi-structured interviews and think-aloud usability sessions with stakeholders at all levels, translating those needs into a semantic search tool that shipped and was integrated with Dropbox.

Between Implications for Design and the Implication Not to Design: Advocating for Artful Reconfiguration

Under review at ACM TOCHI '26

Under Review

Abstract

Instead of always chasing the creation of new tools, we should also focus on rethinking and reshaping the technologies people already use. This paper argues that the more helpful contribution is often to study how people are actually using technology today — not to invent something new. It draws on human psychology and organizational behavior to make the case that the answers often lie in the social and emotional contexts of using technology, not in the mechanics of the system itself. This project pushed me to apply knowledge from the social sciences toward building more human-centered technologies.
