MABLE Indoor Navigation App

Capstone & Independent Study · with Dr. Vinod Namboodiri · Jan 2025 – May 2026

Accessibility-focused smart-glasses concept for blind and low-vision indoor navigation.

The problem

Indoor spaces like hospitals and airport have almost none of the wayfinding infrastructure that outdoor GPS relies on, which makes them especially hard to navigate for blind and low-vision users. I wanted to understand what it would take to build something that actually works here. Through an independent study, I explored how a smart-glasses-based navigation system would take to build.

Methods

  • Competitive analysis of existing navigation apps and AR/smart-glasses hardware
  • Literature review of current wayfinding and low-vision navigation research
  • User profiling across four groups: blind screen-reader users, central vision loss, peripheral vision loss, and mobility impairments
  • Journey mapping to trace how someone actually navigates an unfamiliar building today, and where that breaks down
  • Hardware comparison across candidates like HoloLens 2 and Meta Ray-Ban glasses, on cost, battery life, and accessibility
  • Propose System architecture for required inputs (camera, mic, GPS) and outputs (audio, haptic feedback)
  • Designed a Wizard-of-Oz study to test what users actually need before building anything

Design output

  • Defined core design requirements based on the research findings
  • Compared hardware options on cost, battery life, and accessibility, and selected Meta Ray-Ban glasses
  • Translated pain points from the journey map into specific UI/UX recommendations
  • Wrote technical specifications and a business case for accessibility-focused smart glasses
  • Designed a Wizard-of-Oz study protocol to test what information users actually need, and when, before building anything

Save Tuba — Sustainability Education App

Global Social Impact Fellowship · Lehigh University · Jan – Dec 2023

An educational app that teaches sustainability to young citizens of Kazakhstan.

What

Save Tuba is a gamified sustainability-education app for elementary-school students in Kazakhstan. As a Global Social Impact Fellow, I led the qualitative research behind its redesign. First author, Best Paper Award at GHTC '23.

Methods

  • 6 focus groups with 30+ teachers and students across Kazakh- and Russian-medium schools
  • In-field research during a deployment in Kazakhstan
  • Synthesis of focus-group findings into thematic design insights

Design output

  • Translated field research into new gamification and inquiry-based learning features for the product roadmap
  • Focused the app's curriculum based on teacher feedback and national standards
  • Identified navigation and engagement as key gaps from earlier focus-group feedback, informing the redesign
  • Localized content and features for a multilingual, cross-cultural user base

Semantic Search Tool for a Legal Case Archive

Data, Algorithms and Society Lab, Lehigh University · with Dr. Eric Baumer

What

UX research and design specifications for a semantic search tool over a 3,800+ file legal case archive, which went on to ship integrated into the team's existing Dropbox workflow.

Methods

  • Co-developed interview protocols, analysed and thematically coded 40+ semi-structured interviews and think-aloud usability sessions, with inter-coder reliability checks
  • Identified the work flows, pain points and information needs of stakeholders at every level
  • Reviewed 100+ academic papers across HCI, data visualization, and participatory design

Methods

  • Literature review across HCI, data visualization, and participatory design
  • Semi-structured Interview and workshop protocol design
  • Thematic coding, with an iteratively refined codebook developed across several drafts
  • Pre-interview surveys
  • SWOT analysis
  • Rapid prototyping in Miro and Figma, revised iteratively based on stakeholder feedback

Design output

  • Synthesized and reorganized user needs from interview data
  • Defined functional requirements for the system's analytical/search features
  • Translated stakeholder requirements into UX specifications for search, filtering and navigation

Replicating "Lazy Prices"

Independent computational project · Jan–May 2025

What

This project replicates and extends a landmark Harvard "Lazy Prices" study, testing whether the language companies use in financial disclosures says something about their future stock performance.

Methods

  • Analyzed three decades (1993–2024) of 10-K filings across S&P 500 firms
  • Used cosine similarity to measure how much a company's disclosure language changes year to year
  • Combined text mining (NLP), vector similarity, and financial econometrics to link those textual shifts to future stock returns
  • Built the end-to-end Python pipeline: text processing, similarity scoring, and portfolio construction

Findings

Firms with more stable disclosure language sustained stronger long-term stock performance.

Sentiment Analysis of 10-K Filings

Computational Finance

Methods

  • Applied NLP and dictionary-based sentiment analysis to SEC 10-K filings.
  • Measured overall document sentiment and topic-specific sentiment (Environment, Manufacturing, Technology).
  • Used financial sentiment lexicons to quantify positive and negative language.
  • Evaluated relationships between sentiment and stock returns using event studies and regression analysis.

Outputs & Takeaways

  • Turned unstructured filing text into quantitative sentiment scores and features
  • Built regression models, scatterplots, and correlation heatmaps to visualize the relationship between sentiment and returns
