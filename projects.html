MABLE Indoor Navigation App

Capstone & Independent Study · with Dr. Vinod Namboodiri · January 2025 – May 2026

Accessibility-focused smart-glasses concept for blind and low-vision indoor navigation.

The problem

Indoor spaces like hospitals and airport have almost none of the wayfinding infrastructure that outdoor GPS relies on, which makes them especially hard to navigate for blind and low-vision users. I wanted to understand what it would take to build something that actually works here. I explored how a smart-glasses-based navigation system would take to build.

My Role

Through an independent study, I bridged the user research with concrete hardware and software requirements — what the glasses need to sense and how they should respond — that an engineering team could build against.

Methods

  • Competitive analysis of existing navigation apps and AR/smart-glasses hardware
  • User profiling across four groups: blind screen-reader users, central vision loss, peripheral vision loss, and mobility impairments
  • Journey mapping to trace how someone actually navigates an unfamiliar building today, and where that breaks down
  • Hardware comparison across candidates like HoloLens 2 and Meta Ray-Ban glasses, on cost, battery life, and accessibility

Design output

  • Defined core design requirements based on the research findings
  • Selected Meta Ray-Ban glasses as the hardware platform
  • Translated pain points from the journey map into specific UI/UX recommendations
  • Wrote technical specifications and a business case for accessibility-focused smart glasses
  • Designed a Wizard-of-Oz study to test what information users actually need, and when, before building anything

Save Tuba — Sustainability Education App

Global Social Impact Fellowship · Lehigh University · January 2023 – December 2023

An educational app that teaches sustainability to young citizens of Kazakhstan.

What

Save Tuba is a gamified sustainability-education app for elementary-school students in Kazakhstan. Through field work in Kazakhstan, we gained valuable insights to redesign the app, make it more gamified, apply inquiry-based learning strategies and align with the local culture and standards of education.

My Role

As the Research Lead for the team, I led the qualitative research behind its redesign. I published a first author paper that won the Best Paper Award at IEEE Global Humanitarian Technology Conference '23.

Methods

  • 6 focus groups with users across Kazakh- and Russian-medium schools
  • Ethnographic field work during deployment in Kazakhstan
  • Synthesis of focus-group findings through thematic analysis

Design output

  • Translated field research into new gamification and inquiry-based learning features
  • Identified navigation and engagement as key gaps from focus-group feedback, informing the redesign
  • Focused the app's curriculum based on teacher feedback and national standards
  • Localized content and features for a multilingual, cross-cultural user base

Building For How People Actually Work

Data, Algorithms and Society Lab, Lehigh University · with Dr. Eric Baumer · January 2023 - May 2026

What

Lawyers weren't getting what they needed from the tools they already had, but it wasn't clear whether the fix was a new tool or a better understanding of how they actually worked day to day. We ran interviews to figure out which one it was, and to map their existing habits closely enough to design around them. That research shipped as a working search tool, integrated directly into the team's existing Dropbox workflow.

My Role

As a research assistant, I contributed significantly to data analysis, which led to understanding the user needs and translating those into tasks for the software engineering team. I co-authored 2 papers that explore user feedback ellicitation (Submitted, IJHCI) and a new approach to design (Accepted, CSCW '26).

Methods

  • 40+ Semi-structured interviews and think-aloud sessions to understand user work flows, pain points and information needs of stakeholders at every level
  • Thematic analysis , with a codebook refined across several iterations
  • Rapid prototyping in Miro and Figma, revised based on stakeholder feedback
  • Literature review across HCI, data visualization, and participatory design
  • Survey Design
  • SWOT analysis
  • Workflow analysis
  • Stakeholder analysis

Design output

  • Synthesized and reorganized user needs from interview data
  • Defined functional requirements for the system's analytical/search features
  • Translated stakeholder requirements into UX specifications for search, filtering and navigation

Replicating and Extending the landmark Harvard study "Lazy Prices"

Computational Finance

What

This project replicates and extends a landmark Harvard "Lazy Prices" study, testing whether the language companies use in financial disclosures says something about their future stock performance.

Methods

  • Analyzed three decades (1993–2024) of 10-K filings across S&P 500 firms
  • Combined text mining (NLP) , vector similarity, and financial econometrics to link those textual shifts to future stock returns
  • Built the end-to-end Python pipeline for text processing, similarity scoring, and portfolio construction

Findings

Firms with more stable disclosure language sustained stronger long-term stock performance.

Sentiment Analysis of 10-K Filings

Computational Finance

Methods

  • Turned unstructured filing text into quantitative sentiment scores and features
  • Applied NLP and dictionary-based sentiment analysis to SEC 10-K filings.
  • Calculated and measured overall document sentiment and topic-specific sentiment (Environment, Manufacturing, Technology).
  • Used financial sentiment lexicons to quantify positive and negative language.

Findings

  • Optimistic language was tied to weaker returns and negative language to stronger ones
  • Built regression models, scatterplots, and correlation heatmaps to visualize the relationship between sentiment and returns

    Discrete-Event Simulation for Manufacturing

    Independent research and study · September 2024 – May 2026

    What

    A manufacturing firm that only makes engineered-to-order products needed to understand where its production line actually loses time and money. We built a simulation of their real process, conducted interviews to understand bottlenecks and statistically analysed their data to recommend operational changes.

    Methods

    • Built a deterministic discrete-event simulation model in Arena, using a Future Event List to model production line performance
    • Collected, cleaned, and validated raw machine data from multiple production units
    • Analyzed the data to identify bottlenecks, irregularities, and document the actual process flow
